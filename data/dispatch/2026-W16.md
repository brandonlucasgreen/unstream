An April 14 study landed this week with the cleanest indictment of streaming economics in years: 55% of artists earned less than €1,000 from music in 2025 and 26% earned nothing, while recorded-music revenue hit an all-time high. That's the lead. Pair it with Warner Music quietly buying the B2B rails much of the "indie" sector runs on, plus two new artist-financing products launching within a week, and the picture is clear: the pie grew, and the slice for the bottom 99% did not.

## Platform watch

- **Beatport**: Completed its absorption of Beatsource in March — all subscribers, playlists, and history now live on Beatport. The DJ download/streaming market is now effectively a Beatport monopoly at the professional tier. Unstream's platform listing should reflect the consolidation; Beatsource as a separate entry is dead. [DJ Life](https://djlifemag.com/2026/03/beatport-beatsource-unite-into-one-premium-dj-platform/)
- **Bandcamp**: No product news this week. The **January 13, 2026 ban on music "generated wholly or in substantial part by AI"** is settled policy now — still the only major marketplace with an explicit AI ban on the books. Enforcement criteria remain subjective ("in substantial part" is not quantified), so expect disputes. Bandcamp Friday remains on May 1 (eight Fridays set for 2026). Thirty months into Songtradr ownership, no meaningful change to the artist payout split or core feature set. Stability here matters more than activity. [Bandcamp blog](https://blog.bandcamp.com/2026/01/13/keeping-bandcamp-human/) | [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/bandcamp-bans-ai-generated-music-we-want-musicians-to-keep-making-music/) | [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/12/08/bandcamp-sets-dates-for-eight-bandcamp-friday-promos-in-2026/)
- **Patreon**: 2025 transparency report disclosed podcasters earned **$629M on Patreon (+33% YoY)**. Music creators aren't broken out — they remain the underrepresented sibling to podcasts. Patreon's on-platform discovery push is the thing worth tracking for musicians. [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/patreon-podcaster-earnings-2025-biggest-content-category-1236710779/) | [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/08/patreon-podcast-earnings-2025/)
- **Qobuz**: At Brick Lane Jazz Festival capturing another live album. Hi-res and curation positioning, no structural news. [Qobuz Magazine](https://www.qobuz.com/us-en/magazine/story/2026/04/08/qobuz-at-brick-lane-jazz-festival/)

## Emerging & alternative

- **Subvert.fm**: The co-op's **AI Policy** — drafted over months by elected artist-member board reps Hannah Lee Benson and Iz Ocampo — is now live. Headline rule: no AI-generated music on the platform, with an explicit position that training on co-op members' catalogs without consent is theft. The most concrete co-op-governed AI policy in music to date. Members-only alpha continues to expand through 2026. [Subvert blog](https://subvert.fm/blog/opening-up-subvert-an-update/)
- **OpenWav** + **TuneCore**: Two artist-financing products launched in the same week. **OpenWav Artist Bank** (April 2, Snafu-backed) and **TuneCore Direct Advance** (April 9, with RoyFi) both offer cash advances against future streaming royalties. Useful liquidity, but the trade is a slice of future income — independence financialized into a yield product. [MBW: OpenWav](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/wyclef-jean-and-jaeson-mas-d2c-service-openwav-launches-snafu-backed-indie-artist-financing-platform/) | [MBW: TuneCore/RoyFi](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/tunecore-partners-with-royfi-to-offer-royalty-advances-to-indie-artists/)
- **UMG × Udio**: The licensed AI music platform from UMG's Udio settlement is still in "launching 2026" with no date. Premise — use UMG's catalog as training data, split revenue with rights holders — is the template the industry is converging on. No news this week; track it. [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2025/10/umg-x-udio-settle-launch-licensed-ai-music-platform-in-2026)
- **Mirlo, Ampwall, Faircamp, Jam.coop, Bandwagon, EVEN**: No news this week. Faircamp's core maintainer flagged the main branch may be unstable through summer during a restructuring push — normal for a solo open-source project. Mirlo's public update cadence has slowed since December 2025 — worth a check-in.

## Fediverse & decentralized

- **Funkwhale**: **Funkwhale 2.0** shipped in March (current release 2.0.1) — the biggest release the project has ever put out. New API v2, a fully responsive UI, ListenBrainz sync, multi-artist support, a content quality filter, and a shift from traditional libraries to playlist-centric navigation. Breaking change: v2 pods won't federate with v1 pods, so the fediverse music graph is bifurcated until operators upgrade. This is the most substantive fediverse-music release in years, and a serious alternative for anyone running a self-hosted listening server. The April 1 NLnet ideas deadline passed; whether follow-on funding lands is the next signal. [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/3/funkwhale-2-0-debuts-api-v2-new-responsive-ui-listenbrainz-sync-and-multi-artist-support/) | [Funkwhale changelog](https://docs.funkwhale.audio/changelog.html)
- **Nina Protocol / Sound.xyz**: Quiet week. No signals worth alerting on.

## Streaming economics

- **The paradox study** (April 14): 55% of artists earned less than €1,000 from music in 2025. 26% earned nothing. 83% described themselves as dissatisfied or very dissatisfied with streaming royalty rates. Running parallel to Spotify reporting its largest-ever annual industry payout ($11B in 2025) and recorded-music revenue hitting $31.7B globally. The money is real. It concentrates at the top: 1,500+ artists cleared $1M from Spotify alone, while the median sees fractions of a cent per stream. [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/14/music-streaming-royalties-paradox-study/) | [Axios](https://www.axios.com/media-trends-membership/2026/04/04/streaming-music-payout-gap) | [CelebrityAccess](https://celebrityaccess.com/2026/04/01/173729)
- **Spotify per-stream adjustment**: Spotify's April 2026 rate change — framed as "more artist-forward" — lands around **$0.00318** per secondary trackers, still inside the $0.003–$0.005 band that has held for years. The 1,000-stream rolling-12-month minimum (live since April 2024) remains. The math is essentially unchanged for the long tail. [Chartlex](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/money/how-much-does-spotify-pay-per-stream-2026) | [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-28/2025-music-industry-payouts-whats-next-for-artists/)
- **Spotify video opt-out** (April 9): Spotify rolled out a universal toggle to disable video in the app. Functionally minor — a response to sustained user complaints about autoplay — but signals they're not fully committed to the video pivot. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/09/spotify-now-lets-everyone-turn-off-all-videos-in-its-app/)
- **Deezer AI detection → EJI** (March 27, effects this month): Deezer licensed its AI music detection tool (99.8% accuracy claimed) to Hungarian collecting society EJI, the first PRO to deploy it. The number that matters: **39% of new daily uploads to Deezer are now AI-generated**, up from 28% in September. EJI doesn't pay royalties for AI tracks — that's the policy lever. Deezer is selling the tool to other rights orgs. [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/03/27/deezer-ai-detection-tech-license-eji/) | [Deezer Newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/03/deezer-and-eji-partnership-to-filter-out-recordings-created-by-ai/)

## Industry pulse

- **Warner Music Group → Revelator** (April 1): WMG signed a definitive agreement to acquire **Revelator** — the B2B distribution, rights-management, and royalty-accounting platform that powers a substantial slice of indie infrastructure. The more important ownership story of the month than anything at consumer-facing platforms. A major now owns rails thousands of indie distributors and labels run on. Watch for terms or pricing shifts for indie clients. [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/warner-music-group-agrees-to-acquire-revelator-state-of-the-art-independent-music-platform-302731480.html)
- **Suno × labels**: Suno's licensing talks with UMG and Sony have stalled over whether users can download AI-generated tracks. WMG already settled with both Suno and Udio in late 2025 (Udio's deal forces a "walled garden" remix pivot; Suno's is broader). Sony's cases against both remain active. GEMA's case against Suno has a German ruling scheduled June 12. A US fair-use ruling on AI training data is expected this summer — the most consequential AI-music legal moment of the year. [Winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/04/09/suno-licensing-talks-with-labels-stall-over-ai-music-downloa-xcxwbn/) | [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sunos-licensing-talks-with-major-labels-in-limbo-with-no-path-forward-report/) | [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/music-piracy-ai-lawsuits-top-2026-copyright-litigation-calendar)
- **SAG-AFTRA / AMPTP resume April 27**: AI protections are the central sticking point. The sound-recordings deal already ratified by WMG, Sony, UMG, and Disney Music is the template — AI vocal replicas require consent, minimum compensation, and disclosure. April 27 talks extend those protections to the broader performer contract. [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/04/sag-aftra-resumes-2026-talks-with-amptp-date-1236782965/)
- **87% of artists use AI tools**: A Symphonic Distribution survey (April 8) found nearly 9 in 10 independent artists use AI somewhere in their workflow — mastering/stem separation (79%), songwriting (66%). The frame is increasingly AI-as-tool vs. AI-as-replacement, not AI vs. no-AI. [Symphonic](https://blog.symphonic.com/2026/04/08/ai-tools-independent-musicians-2026/)
- **RIAA 2025**: US recorded-music wholesale hit $11.5B — another record. Latin crossed $1B in the US for the first time. Vinyl still 75%+ of physical revenue. [RIAA](https://www.riaa.com/riaa-reports-us-recorded-music-annual-revenue-achieves-new-high-of-11-5-billion-in-2025/)
- **Musixmatch Sentinel**: New service claiming millisecond-latency detection of copyrighted music or lyrics inside AI-generated content. The detection-as-a-service layer aimed at platforms (not labels) is the interesting move; the latency claim is unverified. [The Business Tempo](https://www.xavierboscher.com/en/the-business-tempo-april-2026-streaming-ai-and-copyright-the-music-industry-at-a-crossroads/)

## Unstream implications

Concrete actions:

1. **Lead with the paradox.** The April 14 data is the clearest empirical case for Unstream's existence: record industry revenue, majority of artists earning almost nothing from it. "In 2025, 55% of artists earned less than €1,000 from streaming" is a stronger homepage/about hook than anything generic about supporting artists. Use it.
2. **Update the Beatport entry.** Beatsource is folded in — listing should reflect the unified platform. Low effort, keeps data accurate.
3. **Surface platform AI policy as a comparison axis.** Subvert (co-op-governed no-AI policy) and Bandcamp (January 2026 ban on AI-generated music) are both on record; Spotify is opaque; Deezer has a detection pipeline but not a ban. Add a one-line AI stance per platform. Pairs with Deezer's 39%-of-uploads-are-AI number — the flood is no longer abstract, and explicit-ban vs. silent vs. detection-only is a real listener-facing differentiator.
4. **Audit which "indie" links route through Revelator.** If WMG owns the rails, users deserve to know which independent-looking distribution paths ride a major-label rail. Identify which distributors in Unstream's search sources integrate Revelator; decide whether that warrants a label.
5. **Skip OpenWav Artist Bank and TuneCore Direct Advance integrations.** Financing products, not artist destinations. Out of scope.
6. **Check in with Mirlo.** Public cadence has slowed. Worth knowing whether the cooperative is funded through 2026 before continuing to recommend them as heavily as we do.
