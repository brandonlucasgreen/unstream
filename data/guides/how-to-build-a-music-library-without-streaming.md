# How to build a music library without streaming

I cancelled my Apple Music subscription after 10+ years. I don't miss it as much as I expected. Here's the practical stuff I figured out along the way.

## Figure out what you actually listen to

Most streaming services show your listening history. Spotify has Wrapped and tools like [stats.fm](https://stats.fm). Apple Music has Replay. If you scrobble with Last.fm or ListenBrainz, you already know.

Make a short list of your top 15–20 artists. Those are the ones to buy from first.

## Start small

You don't need to replace your whole library at once.

- Set a monthly budget. $10–15 is plenty — roughly what streaming costs anyway.
- Buy one or two albums a month from artists you actually love.
- Go for digital downloads (FLAC or MP3) unless you're into vinyl. Cheaper, instant, plays on everything.

Over a year that's 12–24 albums you permanently own, with $100+ going directly to artists.

## Find where artists sell

This used to be annoying. Artists sell across tons of platforms — Bandcamp, Mirlo, Ampwall, their own sites, Ko-fi — and checking each one is tedious.

[Unstream](https://unstream.stream) does this in one search. Type in an artist and it shows everywhere they sell across 23+ platforms, with how much of your money reaches them on each one. Free, no account needed.

## Pick a player

You need something to play your files. Some options:

**Desktop:** Apple Music/iTunes works fine as a local library manager even without a subscription. Strawberry is a solid open-source option. foobar2000 if you're on Windows.

**Mobile:** Apple Music syncs local files on iOS. Poweramp is great on Android. Plexamp if you run a Plex server.

**Self-hosted:** Navidrome or Jellyfin let you run your own streaming server for your purchased music, accessible from anywhere.

Main point: you don't need a subscription to have music on your phone. Local files work.

## Use your library

Your public library probably offers free music through **Hoopla** and **Freegal** — just need a library card. The catalogs are bigger than you'd think. Good for exploring new stuff without spending anything.

## Find music without algorithms

This is honestly one of the better parts of the whole thing:

- **People.** Ask friends, join music forums, subreddits, Discord servers, Mastodon communities.
- **Music blogs.** Bandcamp Daily, The Quietus, genre-specific blogs. Real curation by humans.
- **Radio.** College radio, KEXP, NTS. Goldmines.
- **Shows.** Nothing beats buying a record at the merch table.
- **Artists themselves.** Follow the ones you like — they share music from peers and influences constantly.

## You don't have to be all-or-nothing

Plenty of people keep streaming for casual listening and also buy from the artists they care about most. That's fine. The goal isn't purity — it's shifting more of your spending toward models that actually pay artists.

Start with one album this month from someone you love.
