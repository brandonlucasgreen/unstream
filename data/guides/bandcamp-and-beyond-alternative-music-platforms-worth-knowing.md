# Bandcamp and beyond: alternative music platforms worth knowing

Most people who want to support artists more directly know about Bandcamp. But there's a whole ecosystem of artist-friendly platforms out there, and some of them are really interesting.

## Buy and own

These are platforms where you purchase music — digital downloads, sometimes physical — and the artist keeps most of the money.

**Bandcamp** is the big one. Artists keep 80–85% of every sale, and on [Bandcamp Fridays](https://isitbandcampfriday.com) (first Friday of each month) that jumps to ~97% because Bandcamp waives their cut. Digital downloads, vinyl, merch, subscriptions. Huge catalog. If you like independent music, most of your favorite artists are probably already here.

**Mirlo** is a community-owned cooperative where artists keep 86–90%. It's smaller than Bandcamp but growing, and the ownership model is genuinely different — the platform is governed by its community, not investors.

**Ampwall** is newer and offers artists 92–95%. Clean, simple storefront. The economics are hard to beat.

**Qobuz** sits between streaming and purchasing. You can buy individual albums in hi-res audio (up to 24-bit/192kHz), and artists get about 70%. If you care about sound quality and artist pay, it's a good option. They have a streaming tier too, but buying is where they stand out.

## Self-hosted and decentralized

These push things further — artists run their own infrastructure.

**Faircamp** is an open-source tool that generates a static website for selling music. No platform middleman, so artists keep 90–97% (just payment processor fees). Takes some technical comfort to set up, but the [Faircamp Webring](https://faircamp.webr.ing) helps with discovery.

**Bandwagon** is built on ActivityPub — the same protocol behind Mastodon. A decentralized music marketplace where artists aren't dependent on any single company. Still early, but the idea is good.

## Ongoing support

Not everything has to be a purchase. These let fans support artists on a recurring basis.

**Ko-fi** — artists keep 97% of tips and 92% of sales. Good as a simple "buy me a coffee" link alongside other platforms.

**Patreon** — artists keep 86–90% of membership revenue. Best for musicians who put out regular content — new tracks, behind-the-scenes stuff, early access.

**Buy Me a Coffee** — similar to Ko-fi. Artists get about 92%. Straightforward tipping and monthly support.

## Community platforms

**Jam.coop** is building a listener-owned cooperative streaming service with fairer distribution than Spotify's pro-rata model. Small but the model is compelling.

**Discogs** is the largest physical music marketplace. Great for vinyl, CDs, cassettes. Mostly secondary market (used records), but artists and labels sell new releases through it too.

## Free and legal

Don't sleep on your local library. **Hoopla** and **Freegal** both offer music streaming and downloads with just a library card. The catalogs are bigger than you'd expect, and they're completely free.

## Finding artists on all of this

The biggest pain point with this whole ecosystem is that your favorite artist might be on Bandcamp, Mirlo, *and* Ko-fi — but you'd have no idea unless you checked each one.

That's what [Unstream](https://unstream.stream) does. Search for any artist and it checks 23+ platforms at once, showing you everywhere they sell and how much of your money actually reaches them on each one.
