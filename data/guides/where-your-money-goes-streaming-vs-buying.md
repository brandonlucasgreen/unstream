# Where your money goes when you stream vs. buy music

You pay $10.99 a month for Spotify. You listen to hundreds of songs. Some of that money goes to artists, right?

Sort of. The amount is small enough — and the system weird enough — that it's worth understanding what actually happens to your money before it reaches anyone who made the music.

## How streaming payouts work

Most streaming platforms use a **pro-rata model**. All subscription and ad revenue for a given period goes into one pool. That pool gets divided among rights holders based on their share of *total* streams on the platform.

The key thing: your individual listening doesn't matter. If you exclusively listen to one indie artist all month, your $10.99 doesn't go to them. It gets split based on what everyone on the platform listened to. Artists with hundreds of millions of streams — mostly those on Universal, Sony, and Warner — capture the bulk of that pool.

The commonly cited figure is **$0.003 to $0.005 per stream** on Spotify. An artist needs roughly 250,000 streams to earn $1,000. Most independent musicians are nowhere near that.

## What happens when you buy instead

When you buy music directly on a platform like Bandcamp, Mirlo, or Ampwall, the math is completely different:

| Platform | Artist keeps |
|----------|-------------|
| **Ampwall** | ~92–95% |
| **Ko-fi** | ~92–97% |
| **Mirlo** | ~86–90% |
| **Bandcamp** | ~80–85% |
| **Qobuz** | ~70% |

A $10 album on Bandcamp puts **$8–8.50 in the artist's pocket**. To earn that same amount from Spotify, they'd need about 2,100 streams of a single track.

On Bandcamp Fridays (first Friday of every month), Bandcamp waives their cut entirely. Artists take home ~97% — basically everything minus credit card fees.

## The yearly math

Say you spend $10.99/month on streaming. That's about **$132/year**. Of that, maybe $1–2 reaches the independent artists you actually listen to. The rest goes to the platform, major labels, and the pro-rata pool.

Spend that same $11/month buying one album directly and over a year you've got **12 albums you own** and roughly **$100–110 has gone to the artists who made them**.

And you keep the music. Cancel a streaming subscription and your whole library disappears. Purchased music is yours, on any device, forever.

## Shifting the balance

You don't need to cancel streaming. But even small shifts matter:

- **Buy from one or two artists a month** instead of just streaming them
- **Search on [Unstream](https://unstream.stream)** to find where your favorite artists sell across 23+ platforms and see exactly how much reaches them on each one
- **Buy on Bandcamp Fridays** to maximize what goes to the artist
- **Check your library's music services** — Hoopla and Freegal are free with a library card

Every direct purchase is a small vote for a music economy where the people making the music actually get paid for it.
