You pay $10.99 a month for [Spotify](https://spotify.com). You listen to hundreds of songs. Some of that money goes to artists, right?

Sort of. The amount is small enough — and the system weird enough — that it's worth understanding what actually happens to your money before it reaches anyone who made the music.

## How streaming payouts work

Most streaming platforms use a **pro-rata model**. All subscription and ad revenue for a given period goes into one pool. That pool gets divided among rights holders based on their share of *total* streams on the platform.

The key thing: your individual listening doesn't matter. If you exclusively listen to one indie artist all month, your $10.99 doesn't go to them. It gets split based on what everyone on the platform listened to. So the biggest artists — mostly those on major labels with billions of streams — capture the bulk of that pool, and everyone else gets whatever's left.

There's been talk about switching to a "user-centric" model where your subscription goes to the artists *you* actually listen to, but Spotify and most others still use pro-rata. [Deezer](https://www.deezer.com) has started experimenting with user-centric payouts, and it does seem to help smaller artists, but it's not the norm yet.

The commonly cited figure is **$0.003 to $0.005 per stream** on Spotify. An artist needs roughly 250,000 streams to earn $1,000. Most independent musicians are nowhere near that. And that number has been *declining* over time as the platform adds more subscribers but also more content (including AI-generated tracks competing for the same pool).

## What happens when you buy instead

When you buy music directly on a platform like [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com), [Mirlo](https://mirlo.space), or [Ampwall](https://ampwall.com), the math is completely different. Your money goes to the artist you chose, minus the platform's cut:

| Platform | Artist keeps |
|----------|-------------|
| **[Ampwall](https://ampwall.com)** | ~92–95% |
| **[Ko-fi](https://ko-fi.com)** | ~92–97% |
| **[Mirlo](https://mirlo.space)** | ~86–90% |
| **[Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com)** | ~80–85% |
| **[Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com)** | ~70% |

A $10 album on Bandcamp puts **$8–8.50 in the artist's pocket**. To earn that same amount from Spotify, they'd need about 2,100 streams of a single track. And on Bandcamp Fridays (first Friday of every month), Bandcamp waives their cut entirely — artists take home ~97%, basically everything minus credit card fees.

The other big difference: you own what you buy. You get actual files — MP3, FLAC, WAV, whatever you want — that you can play on any device, any app, forever. No algorithm deciding what you hear next. No platform removing something from your library because of a licensing dispute. Cancel a streaming subscription and your whole library disappears. Purchased music doesn't.

## The yearly math

Say you spend $10.99/month on streaming. That's about **$132/year**. Of that, maybe $1–2 reaches the independent artists you actually listen to — the rest goes to the platform, major labels, and the pro-rata pool.

Spend that same $11/month buying one album directly and over a year you've got **12 albums you permanently own** and roughly **$100–110 that went to the artists who made them**. That's a pretty stark difference.

And it compounds in a way streaming doesn't. After three years of streaming you own nothing and the artists have received almost nothing from you specifically. After three years of buying, you have 36 albums and you've put $300+ directly in artists' hands.

## Shifting the balance

You don't need to cancel streaming — plenty of people keep it for casual listening and convenience while also buying from artists they care about. That's fine. Even small shifts matter:

- **Buy from one or two artists a month** instead of just streaming them. Prioritize the ones who seem like they need it most — independent, unsigned, small following.
- **Search on [Unstream](https://unstream.stream)** to find where your favorite artists sell across 16 platforms and see exactly how much reaches them on each one.
- **Buy on Bandcamp Fridays** to maximize what goes to the artist.
- **Check your library's music services** — [Hoopla](https://www.hoopladigital.com) and [Freegal](https://www.freegalmusic.com) are free with a library card and good for exploring new stuff without spending.

The per-stream payout isn't going to get better anytime soon. But you can route around it.
