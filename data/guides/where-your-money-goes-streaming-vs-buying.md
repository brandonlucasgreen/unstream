# Where your money actually goes: streaming vs. buying music

You pay $10.99 a month for Spotify. You listen to hundreds of songs. Some of that money goes to artists, right?

Technically, yes. But the amount is so small — and the system so tilted — that it's worth understanding what actually happens to your subscription dollar before it reaches the people who made the music you love.

## The streaming royalty pool, explained

Most streaming platforms, Spotify included, use what's called a **pro-rata model**. Here's how it works:

1. All subscription and ad revenue for a given period goes into one big pool.
2. That pool is divided among rights holders based on their share of **total streams** on the platform.
3. Your individual listening habits don't matter. If you exclusively listen to one indie artist all month, your $10.99 doesn't go to them — it gets split based on what *everyone* on the platform listened to.

The result? Artists with hundreds of millions of streams — mostly those signed to the three major record labels (Universal, Sony, and Warner) — capture the vast majority of that pool. Independent artists, even ones with dedicated fanbases, are left with fractions of a penny per stream.

The commonly cited figure is **$0.003 to $0.005 per stream** on Spotify. That means an artist needs roughly 250,000 streams just to earn $1,000. For context, most independent musicians are nowhere near those numbers.

## Who actually benefits?

The streaming model is great for consumers: nearly all recorded music, on demand, for a flat monthly fee. But the economics disproportionately benefit:

- **Major record labels**, who negotiate favorable rates and own massive catalogs
- **Platform shareholders**, who profit from subscriber growth
- **AI-generated content farms**, which flood platforms with cheap tracks designed to capture algorithmic attention and siphon royalty pool money

Small and mid-size independent artists — the ones writing, recording, and producing their own work — are effectively shut out. They earn pennies, get minimal playlist placement, and compete with an ever-growing flood of algorithmically optimized content.

## What happens when you buy instead

When you purchase music directly from an artist on a platform like Bandcamp, Mirlo, or Ampwall, the math changes dramatically:

| Platform | Approximate artist payout |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Ampwall** | ~92–95% of sale price |
| **Ko-fi** | ~92–97% of tips/sales |
| **Mirlo** | ~86–90% of sale price |
| **Bandcamp** | ~80–85% of sale price |
| **Qobuz** | ~70% of sale price |

On Bandcamp Fridays (the first Friday of every month), Bandcamp waives their revenue share entirely, meaning artists take home roughly **97%** of every sale — with only credit card processing fees deducted.

Compare that to streaming: a $10 album purchase on Bandcamp puts **$8–8.50 directly in the artist's pocket**. To earn that same $8.50 from Spotify streams, an artist would need approximately **2,100 plays of a single track**.

## A simple comparison

Let's say you spend $10.99/month on a streaming subscription.

Over a year, that's about **$132**. Of that, maybe $1–2 reaches the independent artists you actually care about — the rest is absorbed by the platform, major labels, and the pro-rata pool.

Now imagine spending that same $11/month buying one album directly. Over a year:

- You've purchased **12 albums** you actually own
- Approximately **$100–110** has gone directly to the artists who made them
- You have a permanent collection that doesn't disappear if you cancel a subscription

## You keep what you buy

This is the part people forget. Streaming is a rental. Cancel your subscription and your entire library vanishes. Every playlist, every saved album — gone.

When you buy music, it's yours. You can play it on any device, any app, forever. No algorithm decides what you hear next. No platform can remove it from your library because of a licensing dispute.

## What you can do

You don't have to go cold turkey on streaming overnight. But you can start shifting some of your music spending toward direct purchases:

- **Pick one or two artists a month** and buy their music instead of just streaming it
- **Check where your favorite artists sell their work** — tools like [Unstream](https://unstream.stream) search 23+ platforms at once so you can find them on Bandcamp, Mirlo, Ampwall, and more
- **Take advantage of Bandcamp Fridays** to maximize what goes to the artist
- **Explore your local library's music services** like Hoopla and Freegal for free listening

The streaming model isn't going to change from the top down. But every direct purchase is a vote for a different kind of music economy — one where the people who make the music actually get paid for it.
