# Voice & Positioning

This is the single source of truth for Unstream's brand voice, messaging, and platform-specific content guidelines. Reference this doc whenever generating any content.

---

## Positioning

### Core message

Unstream is a free tool that helps fans support artists directly on alternative platforms and reduce their dependency on streaming.

### Primary tagline (as shown on site)

> Support artists directly on alternative platforms.
> Reduce your dependency on streaming.

### The Robin Hood angle

Unstream is completely free. No paywall, no premium tier, no data harvesting. It exists to redirect money from platforms that keep 70% to platforms where artists keep 82%+. The product itself is donation-ware — you can support its development if you want, but you never have to. This is a tool built by a musician to help other musicians get paid. That story is the brand.

### Why this positioning works

* Leads with the action fans already want to take (supporting artists)
* "Alternative platforms" signals there's a world beyond Spotify without demanding you quit
* "Reduce your dependency" is honest and realistic — not asking anyone to go cold turkey, just shift the balance
* Unstream as the enabler, not the product — it's a means to an end

### Positioning statement (internal)

> For music fans who care about supporting artists, Unstream is a free, open-source search tool that shows where to buy, stream, and support any artist across 13+ alternative platforms — with transparent payout percentages so you know exactly where your money goes. Unlike streaming apps that bury alternatives, Unstream puts artist-friendly options front and center. It's completely free because the whole point is getting more money to artists, not less.

### Messaging hierarchy

| Priority | Message | Where to use |
| -------- | ------- | ------------ |
| 1 | Support artists better ("see where your money goes") | Headlines, extension store listings, social |
| 2 | Completely free — no catch, no paywall | Subheadlines, trust signals, extension listings |
| 3 | 13+ platforms searched in one place | Feature lists, product descriptions |
| 4 | Open source, no tracking | Trust signals, footer, about page |
| 5 | Built by a musician | Story/about page, PR, social bios |
| 6 | Works with Spotify/Apple Music (extension) | Platform-specific landing sections |

### Donation framing

Frame donating as supporting the mission, not buying features. "If Unstream helped you find a new way to support an artist, consider keeping the lights on." Bandcamp Friday is a natural moment for this — the giving mindset is already there.

## Brand voice

Derived from analysis of 24+ blog posts (2015–2026), social posts, and Slack messages.

### The core thing

Brandon writes like someone who is genuinely figuring something out, not someone who already has. This is not a stylistic choice — it is the actual mechanism. The thinking happens in real time on the page. He follows his own curiosity from a specific personal observation outward to something bigger, and he doesn't always know where he's going when he starts. Sometimes he doesn't end up anywhere in particular, and that's fine.

Everything else in this document flows from that. If you get that right, a lot of the other things follow naturally. If you get that wrong, no amount of correct punctuation or sentence rhythm will save it.

### Voice traits

**Dry and sarcastic.** Humor is deadpan. It arrives in parentheticals or brief asides that undercut the seriousness of what came before. He doesn't set up jokes. He just says something dry and moves on. The reader is trusted to notice.

**Self-deprecating without being self-pitying.** He'll call his early music "absolute garbage" or acknowledge that his blog is obscure — but it's stated flatly, not as a bid for reassurance. There's no fishing happening.

**Opinionated and direct.** When he has a conviction, he says it. "I despise it." "You don't need to worry at all about appealing to the vast majority of people." He doesn't wrap strong feelings in qualifiers. But he frames opinions as his own perspective, not as objective analysis. "I think" and "it seems to me" rather than "the reality is."

**Comfortable with ambiguity.** Not every piece resolves. He ends on open questions. He admits he doesn't know. This isn't weakness — it's the point.

**Personal details woven into analysis.** He'll move from talking about the music industry to mentioning his daughter's bedtime without fanfare. The macro and the micro coexist, and the transitions aren't announced.

### Sentence & paragraph rhythm

Sentences vary significantly in length. A long, winding sentence with a parenthetical or two will be followed by something short and punchy. This mirrors speech.

Paragraphs are short — usually 1–4 sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs are common, especially for emphasis or rhetorical questions.

He starts sentences with "But," "And," and "So" regularly. This is how people actually think.

Rhetorical questions do real structural work — they appear as standalone paragraphs and genuinely redirect where the piece goes next:
> "Why is that?"
> "Am I depressed? Maybe I'm depressed."
> "Am I just too risk-averse to go look for it?"

Parenthetical asides are heavy and functional — they add humor, caveats, self-awareness, or real-life detail.

### How pieces are structured

**Openings** don't start with a thesis. They start with something specific — a personal anecdote, a direct admission, a scene, an observation.

**The body** follows the shape of actual thinking: here's a thing I noticed → here's what I think about it → but actually there's this other angle → I'm not sure → here's where I've landed (maybe).

**Headers** appear in longer pieces when there's a genuine topic shift, but not always. When they do appear, they're plain and casual — sentence case, non-parallel, more like labels than chapter titles. "Some guiding principles." "The tools." "What I actually did."

**Endings** don't summarize. The last line is a thought, a question, an observation, or a dry one-liner. Sometimes the piece just stops when the thought feels complete.

### Formatting rules

**Bold** for thesis statements or key convictions. One or two per section, max.

**Italics** for single-word or short-phrase emphasis — to make you hear the stress in his voice: "_really_ hard," "I _can_ still do this."

**Links** woven into sentences naturally. Not called out.

**Lists** only for genuinely enumerable things — not for explaining ideas or making arguments. List items are substantial: full sentences minimum.

**Strikethrough** occasionally for humorous corrections: "~~end of days~~ end of the year."

### Emotional register

Brandon's emotions are real, but he never narrates or announces them. He doesn't say "if I'm being honest" — he just is honest, and the reader picks it up from the content itself.

**Vulnerability comes through in the shape of the thinking**, not through explicit declarations. When he's conflicted, the paragraph structure wanders between two positions. When he's excited, the specificity increases and sentences get shorter. When he's frustrated, the writing gets drier and more clipped. The emotion is in the prose texture.

## Short-form social voice

Different register from long-form. Shorter, punchier, often a single thought with no elaboration. The exploratory quality is still there but compressed.

### Casing as a register signal

This is a real and consistent pattern: **lowercase-everything signals off-the-cuff; normal casing signals something more considered.** These aren't typos — they're different modes.

Off-the-cuff (lowercase):
- "i am starting to think that maybe, just maybe, the ai ceo's are just engaging in market manipulation"
- "messing around with verified artist pages on unstream 👀"

More considered (normal casing):
- "Somewhat hot take: I think every small indie musician should consider just giving all their music away for free"

### Emoji

Emoji are used on social — sparingly and functionally. 🤘 for excitement, 👀 for "I'm up to something," 🥴 for self-aware absurdity, 😄 for genuine enthusiasm, 👇 as a CTA pointer. One per post is the ceiling; many posts have none. Especially appropriate on Threads and LinkedIn. Never decorative, never stacked.

### Hashtags

Bluesky gets hashtags for music/creator community discoverability: `#musicsky`, `#fairtrademusic`, `#indiemusic`, `#newmusic`, `#indie`. Placed at the end, not woven mid-sentence.

Threads uses "Tags" (one per post). Use tags like `Music Threads` or `musicians of threads` when relevant.

Mastodon relies on hashtags for discovery. 3-5 relevant tags, CamelCase for accessibility (#BandcampFriday not #bandcampfriday).

LinkedIn: 3-5 industry tags (#MusicIndustry #CreatorEconomy #IndieMusic #SupportArtists).

### Post types

**Pure observation / non-sequitur.** No setup, no punchline, just the thing:
- "Made a vegetarian risotto for dinner"
- "Might be out of my songwriting block"

**Dry take.** A flat, deadpan observation on something happening:
- "pov: you decide to post your app on product hunt and it's *not* AI or fintech"

**Hear me out.** Sets up a take that might seem weird, then invites you into the reasoning — but doesn't close the loop in the post itself:
- "Somewhat hot take: I think every small indie musician should consider just giving all their music away for free... Hear me out:"

**Update / announcement (sideways).** He doesn't announce things head-on. The framing is always personal-stakes-first, not "here's my product":
- "I sometimes forget that I built an app 2 months ago"
- "Fun update: I made an app over the holiday break!"
- "Tiny numbers but still kinda fun:" (sharing stats with self-deprecating framing)

**In-process / thinking out loud.** Fragment of something unresolved:
- "i have a very big and fun idea for Unstream I now really badly want to work on"

**Appreciation / discovery post.** Genuine, unsolicited, no agenda:
- "A little ashamed it took Bad Bunny playing the Super Bowl for me to realize just how talented and interesting he is"

### What doesn't happen on social

No "thoughts?" engagement bait. No "RT if you agree." No "thread 🧵" dramatics. No "this is important" framing. If he asks a question, it's because he actually wants to think about it.

He doesn't narrate himself admiringly. "just wrapped up a marathon session in the studio feeling incredibly grateful 🙏" — this never happens. If he finished something, you get: "Might be out of my songwriting block." That's it.

## Platform-specific guidelines

### Active platforms

Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, LinkedIn. **Twitter/X is not used** — ethical decision, not an oversight.

### Threads
* **Character limit:** 500 per post
* **Tone:** The most personal and unstructured. Single observations, half-formed questions, dry asides. Posts should feel like a thought shared with people you're already in conversation with.
* **Hashtags:** 0-1. Use Tags feature when relevant.
* **Emoji:** Yes, sparingly.
* **Best times:** Evenings (7-9 PM ET), weekends

### Bluesky
* **Character limit:** 300 per post
* **Tone:** Casual, direct. Tighter than Threads because of the character limit. Lead with the hook.
* **Hashtags:** End-of-post community tags (#musicsky, #fairtrademusic, etc.)
* **Emoji:** Sparingly.
* **Best times:** Midday and evenings
* **Notes:** Link in post body works.

### Mastodon
* **Character limit:** 500 (default; varies by instance)
* **Tone:** Can be slightly more detailed. Mastodon users appreciate context and transparency.
* **Hashtags:** 3-5 relevant tags, CamelCase (#BandcampFriday). Mastodon relies on hashtags for discovery.
* **Emoji:** Sparingly.
* **Best times:** Midday (12-2 PM ET), evenings. Active FOSS/indie community.
* **Notes:** Alt text on images. This audience values the open/indie ethos.

### LinkedIn
* **Character limit:** 3,000 per post
* **Tone:** Still personal, but framed around creator economy, fair pay, music industry context. Not corporate. Best platform for the streaming economics angle and longer-form pieces.
* **Hashtags:** 3-5 industry tags (#MusicIndustry #CreatorEconomy #IndieMusic)
* **Emoji:** Yes, sparingly. More acceptable here than other platforms.
* **Best times:** Tue-Thu, 8-10 AM ET or 12 PM ET
* **Notes:** 1-2x/week max.

### Cross-platform rules
* Always include `unstream.stream` link on announcement posts
* No "disrupting" or "revolutionizing" — say what the thing actually does
* No fake urgency or growth-hack language
* Screenshots welcome but not required — text-first

## What not to do

These are patterns that will immediately make the writing not sound like Brandon. They are also common AI writing patterns, which makes them doubly worth avoiding.

**Don't summarize at the end.** No "In conclusion," "Overall," "To sum up."

**Don't make authoritative pronouncements.** "The real X is..." "What's actually happening is..." — these position the writer as an expert delivering a verdict from above. Brandon writes from alongside the reader. If a sentence could work as a TED talk pull-quote, rewrite it.

**Don't narrate your own honesty or vulnerability.** This is the single biggest tell of AI writing trying to sound personal. The honesty is in the content and the messiness — not in a label.

**Don't open with throat-clearing.** No "In today's rapidly changing landscape," no warm-up sentences that aren't saying anything. Start with something concrete.

**Don't perform balance you don't feel.** Brandon has opinions. He acknowledges complexity, but he doesn't pretend to be neutral when he isn't.

**Don't write one-word sentences.** "Real." "Exactly." "Same." — stylistic affectations he doesn't use.

**Don't use neat parallel section headers.** Brandon's headers, when they appear at all, are casual non-parallel labels in sentence case.

**Don't title in title case or as hooks.** Sentence case, plain, short. "Niches vs subcultures." Not "Niches vs. Subcultures: Why the Distinction Matters."

**Don't overuse filler intensifiers.** "Actually," "really," "just," "honestly," "literally" — use sparingly.

**Never use these words/phrases:**
- "leverage" (as a verb), "utilize," "robust," "comprehensive," "holistic"
- "game-changer," "cutting-edge," "innovative"
- "delve," "dive deep," "unpack"
- "it's worth noting," "at the end of the day"
- "if I'm being honest," "I'll admit," "that's not nothing"
- "and that's okay," "and honestly? I think that's fine"

## Topic-specific notes

**Music / Kid Lightbulbs:** He writes about music from inside the experience of making it independently, at small scale, with clear eyes. He values a few true fans over any metric. Critical of streaming platforms and gatekeeping. Advocates transparency and sharing knowledge among independent artists.

**Technology / independence:** Gravitates toward self-hosted, open-source, DIY solutions. Skeptical of big platforms but pragmatic — he'll use them when necessary.

**AI:** Nuanced. Critical of the industry's environmental and ethical impacts, deeply values human creative expression, but acknowledges specific practical uses (coding assistance, etc.). Tired of relitigating the debate, but still has strong feelings about the creative side.

**Family:** Mentioned warmly but briefly. Grounding details, not extended narrative.
