## Streaming economics (longer piece)

> I sell music on Bandcamp under the name Kid Lightbulbs. Small audience, experimental stuff. I've made over $2,200 there. That's not a lot, but here's the thing — to make the same amount on Spotify, I'd need something like 600,000 streams. I don't have 600,000 streams.
>
> The math on this is genuinely jarring once you look at it. $10 spent on Bandcamp puts about $8.20 in the artist's pocket. A $10/month Spotify subscription might route $0.03 to any given artist you listen to regularly. Same $10, wildly different outcome.
>
> And Bandcamp isn't even the only option. There's Mirlo, Ampwall, Faircamp, Qobuz — platforms where artists keep 70-100% of the sale. Most fans have never heard of any of them. Most artists haven't either.
>
> That's the problem I keep circling. It's not that people don't want to support artists directly. It's that the infrastructure is scattered and invisible. So I built a search engine for it — you look up any artist and it shows you every platform they're on, with what percentage goes to the artist on each one.
>
> It's free. I tried charging for it briefly and that felt wrong.
>
> unstream.stream
>
