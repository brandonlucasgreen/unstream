Unstream Dispatch

Weekly Unstream Dispatch, week of August 9

August 9, 2026

Platform watch

  • Bandcamp: Rolling out several meaningful updates. Added a Subsonic API implementation (beta) so fans can stream their Bandcamp collections through clients like Amperfy, Feishin, and Submariner. Added a "filter by artists you follow" feature in Discover. Launched built-in shipping label purchase/printing via EasyPost for US and Canadian sellers. Added publishing rights fields (songwriter, publisher, ISWC) to track/album metadata. Also declared a hardline AI policy: music "generated wholly or in substantial part by AI" is banned. Still rolling out the Stripe payment migration from PayPal. No sign of stagnation under Songtradr — these are real product moves, not just maintenance. Sources: blog.bandcamp.com
  • Spotify: Q2 2026 earnings hit 300M Premium subscribers and 777M MAUs, €4.8B revenue (+14% Y/Y), gross margin 33.4%. The big story this week: signed Kobalt as the third major rights holder (after UMG and Merlin) for its upcoming AI-powered fan-made covers and remixes tool, which will be a paid Premium add-on. Participation is opt-in per songwriter, but the framing around "consent, credit, compensation" is Spotify's attempt to get ahead of the AI trust deficit. Also launched "Talk to Spotify" conversational AI beta for Premium users and expanded AI DJ to 75+ markets. Sources: newsroom.spotify.com, routenote.com
  • Deezer: Partnered with Winamp to power a new premium streaming service inside the next-gen Winamp Player, launching H1 2027. Winamp claims 40M+ active desktop users. This is a white-label deal — Deezer provides the catalogue and infrastructure, Winamp builds the consumer brand. Interesting because it reunites local-library and streaming listening in one app, which is a niche Spotify doesn't serve well. Sources: routenote.com

Emerging & alternative

  • Vocana: Entered public beta roughly a year ago (Aug 2025) with user-centric payouts and CD Baby/DistroKid distribution deals. Still in beta as of early 2026. Music Ally's take was blunt: "inventive product development and smart marketing on a budget unlikely to match the Spotifys of the world will be required." No major updates found this week. The user-centric payout model remains Vocana's differentiator, but adoption signals are quiet.
  • Ampwall: Public benefit company, ~1,000+ bands and artists on the platform, $10/year for artists, 5% cut + PayPal fees. Positioning as the anti-Bandcamp for artists who want lower fees and modern UX under artist/DIY ownership. No new developments this week.
  • Mirlo: Open-source, prix-libre (pay-what-you-want) model, building toward federated infrastructure. Interview earlier this year described it as "the Bandcamp alternative building federative" tools. Still early, still niche, but the open-source angle is genuinely different from Ampwall's approach.

Fediverse & decentralized

  • Funkwhale: Still active as the main ActivityPub music platform, but no significant news or releases this week. Community on Reddit continues to discuss it primarily as self-hosted personal music storage/NAS rather than a discovery platform. The gap between Funkwhale's capabilities and what artists actually need (audience, commerce) remains wide.
  • No new ActivityPub music projects emerged this week.

Streaming economics

  • Spotify price hikes continuing: The Aug 2025 global price increases (US went $10.99→$11.99, then $11.99→$12.99 in Jan 2026) are now settled in. More revenue per sub, but per-stream rates haven't meaningfully improved for artists — labels and Spotify still take their cut first.
  • PRS for Music moved to monthly streaming royalties (effective Aug 15, 2025). UK songwriters and publishers now get paid monthly instead of quarterly for streaming. This is a structural win for cash flow. If you're UK-based and collecting PRS, this matters.
  • Indie bands quitting Spotify: The "Spotify exodus of 2025" saw Deerhoof, Xiu Xiu, Godspeed You! Black Emperor, King Gizzard, and Hotline TNT all pull music — triggered by Daniel Ek's €600M investment in Helsing, an AI defense company. GY!BE went further, leaving all platforms except Bandcamp. Source: The Guardian
  • Instagram locked livestreaming behind 1,000+ followers, cutting off small artists from a key discovery tool.

Industry pulse

  • IFPI + major/indie labels propose AI music chart rules (Aug 10, 2026): A coalition including Sony, UMG, WMG, Believe, BMG, Concord, HYBE, Mom+Pop and others proposed that AI-assisted recordings should only chart if they (1) use authorized AI services, (2) are "substantially human-made," and (3) don't raise chart manipulation concerns. IFPI rolled out three core principles globally. The "substantially human-made" language is deliberately vague — expect fights over where the line sits. Sources: routenote.com
  • Suno published "responsible AI" principles (Aug 10, 2026): Download limits to prevent mass distribution to streaming platforms, audio watermarking and fingerprinting, updated community guidelines prohibiting impersonation and unauthorized uploads. This follows GEMA's copyright lawsuit win against Suno in a German court. Suno says it's trying to draw a line between creative tool and mass-production pipeline, but the unresolved question remains: what copyrighted music trained their models? Sources: routenote.com
  • Deezer: AI-generated tracks now >50% of new daily uploads at peak (~90,000/day), but only 1–3% of actual streams. 85% of streams on fully AI tracks were fraudulent in 2025. This is the data point that justifies the chart rule proposals.
  • Global music industry revenue hit $47.1B in 2025 (8.6% growth), driven by streaming. Paid streaming subscriptions reached 856M–921M depending on the metric. Source: mediaplaynews.com

Unstream implications

  • Bandcamp's Subsonic API is a trust move. Letting fans stream their purchased collections through third-party clients means Bandcamp is choosing interoperability over lock-in. If Unstream supports or integrates with Subsonic-compatible protocols, it's a natural alignment with the same philosophy.
  • The AI chart rules + Suno watermarking + Deezer tagging = a emerging infrastructure for "AI transparency." Unstream should consider how it wants to label or filter AI-generated content now, before external frameworks impose it. Being proactive on provenance metadata (human-made, AI-assisted, AI-generated) positions you well.
  • Spotify's AI covers/remixes tool is going live. It's a paid Premium feature with opt-in from rightsholders. This creates a new category of "fan-made derivative content" that lives inside Spotify's ecosystem. If Unstream offers any remix/derivative features, the contrast in who controls it and who gets paid matters.
  • The indie Spotify boycott set a precedent. Five notable bands left Spotify over ethics, not economics. GY!BE stayed only on Bandcamp. This validates the market for artist-first, ethics-transparent platforms. Unstream's value prop isn't just "better economics" — it's "you don't have to compromise your values."
  • Winamp + Deezer is a reminder that "local files + streaming" is underserved. Listeners who own music and want it alongside streaming catalog have no great option from the majors. If Unstream has any local-collection or library-management features, that's a real differentiator.

Confidence

  • Confirmed: Spotify Q2 2026 numbers, Spotify-Kobalt deal, Deezer-Winamp partnership, IFPI chart principles, Suno responsible AI announcement, Bandcamp feature updates, GEMA v. Suno ruling, Deezer AI upload stats, PRS monthly payouts, indie band Spotify exits.
  • Likely but unverified: Vocana is still in beta without meaningful adoption updates. Ampwall user counts (~1,000 bands) come from their own marketing and may be generous.
  • Speculation: Whether Spotify's AI covers/remixes tool will meaningfully change fan behavior or become a novelty. Whether the Winamp + Deezer product actually ships on time in H1 2027.
