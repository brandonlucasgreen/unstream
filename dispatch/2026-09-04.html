Unstream Dispatch

Weekly Unstream Dispatch, week of September 4

September 4, 2026

Platform watch

  • Bandcamp: Bandcamp Friday today (Sep 4). Otherwise a quiet week — no new feature launches, policy changes, or ownership news. The post-Songtradr hangover persists: the engineering team is reportedly decimated, and the platform continues to coast on brand equity and inertia rather than investment. The Bandcamp Daily editorial is still publishing, which is something, but the platform hasn't shipped meaningful product updates in months. For Unstream, Bandcamp's stasis is both a vulnerability (artists stuck on a declining platform) and an opportunity (artists are actively looking elsewhere). Source: blog.bandcamp.com
  • SoundCloud: Big move — SoundCloud launched a direct music sales beta on Aug 26, letting ~200 US-based Artist Pro creators sell downloads directly from their profiles with zero commission. Fans buy and download without leaving SoundCloud. This is a direct shot at Bandcamp's core value prop. SoundCloud already has 250K+ artists linking 2.4M tracks to external storefronts, sending 500K+ users off-platform annually to buy music. This closes that loop. If rolled out fully, it makes SoundCloud a two-sided market (streaming + direct sales) that could siphon artists who've been tolerating Bandcamp's lack of development. Separately, SoundCloud acquired Nina Protocol (see Emerging section). Sources: musicbusinessworldwide.com, weraveyou.com
  • Ampwall: Still active, still community-focused. A July 2026 blog post explicitly positions Ampwall as "an alternative to Bandcamp" — lower fees, public benefit company structure, direct dev team access. Claiming 1,000+ bands and thousands of registered fans. No major updates this week. The DIY community testimonials are genuine, but scale remains tiny. Source: ampwall.com
  • Mirlo: Quiet week for releases, but the FairPlayer integration (announced Aug 10) is the most interesting federated music story right now. Mirlo artists who opt in can have their music discoverable alongside Faircamp artists in FairPlayer, a federated streaming front-end. It's not a separate streaming service — more like a "food court" where listeners sample from multiple indie stores. This is exactly the kind of interoperability Unstream should be watching closely. Default platform cut is 10%, artists can set it to 0%. Source: mirlo.space
  • Qobuz / Beatport / Patreon / Discogs: No significant news this week.

Emerging & alternative

  • SoundCloud acquires Nina Protocol: Announced Jul 22. Nina Protocol shut down after five years, admitting it couldn't find "a path to sustainability at its current size." SoundCloud bought what was left — primarily the editorial archive, genre map, and artist migration pipeline. Nina artists get free SoundCloud Artist Pro through end of 2026. Nina's team is not transferring over. Key takeaway: even the most principled blockchain music platform couldn't sustain itself. The "100% royalties + community revenue share" model didn't pencil out. Source: musically.com, pitchfork.com
  • Sound.xyz: Gone. Site now says "Sound.xyz is offline as of January 16, 2026." The NFT minting platform pivoted to a subscription product called Sound Premium before shutting down entirely. Another Web3 music casualty. Source: sound.xyz
  • Subvert.fm: Publicly launched May 12, 2026, as a cooperatively-owned music marketplace. Now claims 29,000+ member-owners. It's a D2C sales platform (not streaming), structured as a Colorado cooperative. Artists and labels sell digital music and merch directly; supporters can become co-owners. Early days, but the co-op model is gaining real traction as a differentiator from VC-backed alternatives. Worth watching. Source: subvert.fm, Wikipedia
  • EVEN: The UMG-backed superfan platform. In February 2026, EVEN signed a multi-year deal with Universal Music Group to serve as UMG's D2C infrastructure, letting UMG artists sell music, merch, and experiences directly through EVEN. EVEN claims 500K+ onboarded artists and 3,000+ labels. The UMG deal gives EVEN massive label-side reach, but it's fundamentally a major-label tool, not an indie play. Source: musicbusinessworldwide.com
  • Vocana: Still in public beta. The "indie-only streaming platform" with user-centric payouts and social discovery features. Partnerships with Symphonic and CD Baby for early access. Forbes named it as one of the "specialized music services" to watch. No major launch or funding news this week. Source: vocana.co, forbes.com
  • Ampled: Dead. Closed end of 2023. (Confirming for the record.)
  • Resonate: Dead. Closed before Ampled.

Fediverse & decentralized

  • Mirlo + FairPlayer: Covered above. This is the real story. FairPlayer acts as a federated discovery layer, pulling music from both Mirlo and Faircamp instances into one player. It's a working proof-of-concept for ActivityPub-style music interoperability — exactly the model Unstream should be studying (or joining). Source: mirlo.space, fairplayer.org
  • Funkwhale: No significant news this week. The project remains active but niche — a self-hosted, federated music platform primarily used for personal libraries and freely licensed content. Not a commercial storefront option. Source: funkwhale.audio
  • Jam.coop: The cooperatively-owned music store is live and functional. Small catalog, community-driven. Sits alongside Mirlo, Subvert, and Ampwall in the growing "music co-op" ecosystem. Source: jam.coop

Streaming economics

  • RIAA Mid-Year 2026: US recorded music revenue hit $6 billion in H1 2026, up 6.9% YoY. Streaming remains 82% of total revenue at $4.9B (+4.7%). Paid subscriptions grew 5.5% in units to 111.1M and 7.8% in value to $3.1B. Physical formats also up: vinyl +17.7% to $554M, CDs +58.6% to $171M. The CD spike is notable — Luminate reported a 16% increase in CD unit sales too. Downloads down 12.7%. Source: billboard.com, musicrow.com
  • Spotify AI Persona badge: Rolling out mid-September. Synthetic artist profiles will get labeled "Likely AI Persona" and excluded from editorial and algorithmic recommendations by default unless a listener already follows them. This is distinct from Apple's approach — Spotify targets *artist identity*, not *use of AI tools*. Real musicians using AI in production are not affected. This is a significant policy move: it effectively quarantines AI-generated artist accounts from discovery. Source: routenote.com/blog
  • Apple Music "Made With AI" labels: Coming later in 2026. Labels and distributors must disclose when AI created a "material portion" of content. Covers AI-generated music, artwork, and music videos. Different philosophy from Spotify — Apple is tagging *content*, not *identity*. Source: routenote.com/blog
  • Spotify x Merlin fan-made remixes: Spotify signed a licensing deal with Merlin (indie label/distributor body) on Aug 4 for its upcoming AI-powered fan remix tool, extending beyond the existing UMG deal. Kobalt also signed on Aug 13. Premium subscribers will be able to create licensed covers and remixes, with participating songwriters/artists sharing in the revenue. Source: newsroom.spotify.com
  • Spotify per-stream rate: Still averaging ~$0.004/stream in 2026. No changes. Source: artyfile.com
  • YouTube Partner Program: Major threshold increases coming Feb 2027 — 8,000 watch hours (up from 4,000) or 20M Shorts views (up from 10M) to monetize. Shorts creators must maintain 10M views per 90 days to keep earning. This will squeeze smaller music creators on YouTube. Source: routenote.com/blog
  • SoundCloud direct sales: 0% commission on direct track sales (beta, ~200 artists). Payment processing fees still apply (Stripe). If this scales, it's a meaningful alternative to Bandcamp's 10-15% cut. Source: weraveyou.com

Industry pulse

  • Sony Music Publishing & Warner Chappell sue Anthropic: Filed Aug 28 in N. District of California. Multi-billion-dollar lawsuit alleging mass copyright infringement — claiming Anthropic used tens of thousands of musical compositions (including "All I Want for Christmas Is You," "Eye of the Tiger," "Uptown Funk") to train Claude. Seeking up to $150K per work infringed. This joins existing suits from UMG, Concord, and BMG. Anthropic previously settled with authors for $1.5B. The question at the heart: can AI companies train on copyrighted works without a license? Source: routenote.com/blog
  • Jason Isbell class action against Suno: Filed Sep 3. Artists continue to litigate against generative AI music platforms. Source: musicrow.com
  • Deezer AI upload data: Over 50% of new daily uploads to Deezer are now identified as AI-generated. But AI tracks account for only 1-3% of actual streams. The volume-to-engagement gap is the key stat here. Source: routenote.com/blog (via Deezer)
  • Germany grassroots ticket levy pilot: A national pilot program for a grassroots-led ticket levy system launched in late August — potentially a model for other markets to fund small venues. Source: iqmagazine.com

Unstream implications

  • FairPlayer/Mirlo federation is the model to study or join. The federated discovery layer — where listeners browse music across multiple independent storefronts in one interface — is exactly what the indie music ecosystem needs. If Unstream isn't already talking to the Mirlo/FairPlayer folks, start. If Unstream can expose its catalog via FairPlayer or a similar ActivityPub protocol, it gets free federated discovery.
  • SoundCloud's zero-commission direct sales beta is the competitive benchmark. SoundCloud is now offering what Bandcamp charges 10-15% for, at 0% (minus processing). Unstream's pricing model needs to be clear about why artists should pay Unstream instead of using SoundCloud's free option. Community, curation, and fediverse integration are the differentiators — not price.
  • AI labeling is becoming table stakes. Both Spotify and Apple are rolling out AI transparency features this fall. If Unstream accepts user uploads, it should have an AI disclosure policy ready — ideally one that matches or exceeds the major platforms' standards. Being ahead on transparency builds trust with the artist community.
  • The co-op wave is real. Subvert (29K members, just launched May 2026), Jam.coop, Mirlo — the cooperative ownership model for music platforms is gaining momentum. Unstream should consider how its governance model positions it relative to this movement. If Unstream isn't a co-op, it should be able to articulate why not without sounding like a VC pitch.
  • Web3 music is dead. Sound.xyz: offline. Nina Protocol: acquired for parts by SoundCloud. Audius token: down 95%. Royal.io: shut down. OneOf: collapsed. The 2021-2022 Web3 music cycle burned $300M+ in VC and produced zero sustainable businesses. Unstream should not touch tokens, NFTs, or blockchain-based royalty schemes. The artists who won during that window used Spotify + Bandcamp + email, not blockchain.
  • CDs are back (?). A 58.6% increase in CD revenue is weird and worth monitoring. Probably a collector/hobbyist phenomenon rather than a structural shift, but physical format diversification matters for Unstream if it ever handles merch.
  • Spotify's fan remix tool is expanding fast. With UMG, Merlin, and now Kobalt all signed on, Spotify is building a licensed remix ecosystem. This could become a discovery and engagement vector. Unstream should think about whether fan remixing/covers fit its model — and if so, how to do it without requiring artists to opt into Spotify's ecosystem.
