Weekly Unstream Dispatch, week of September 7

September 7, 2026

Platform watch

  • Bandcamp: Quiet week. September 4 was a Bandcamp Friday — no changes to platform pricing, policy, or ownership to report. The Songtradr era continues without notable incident or improvement. (daily.bandcamp.com)
  • Ampwall: Growing steadily, now claims 1,000+ bands/artists. Still a public benefit company, artist-owned, $10/yr for 5 hours of audio, 5% transaction cut. The community angle (Discord integration, direct dev access) remains its differentiator vs. Bandcamp's corporate neglect. Recent blog post explicitly positions itself as "the Bandcamp alternative" post-Songtradr. (ampwall.com, hypebot.com)
  • SoundCloud: Launched direct music sales in beta (Aug 27), rolling out widely this fall. This is a direct shot at Bandcamp's storefront model from a platform with massive existing reach. Worth watching whether the revenue split is competitive. (newindustryfocus.com)
  • Notes.fm: Expanded this week with Stripe-powered financial accounts and a new "Releases" feature streamlining distribution and publishing admin. Moving toward becoming a full artist business stack, not just a tool. (newindustryfocus.com)

Emerging & alternative

  • Nina Protocol: Dead. The Solana-based web3 music platform announced it's shutting down and merging into SoundCloud. Artists can migrate releases via a tool on Nina's site and get a free SoundCloud Artist Pro subscription through year-end. The editorial archive will be "preserved" by SoundCloud. This is the highest-profile web3 music shutdown yet — Nina had real community and curation. The exit validates the thesis that token-gated music alone doesn't sustain a platform; distribution beats ideology. (ninaprotocol.com)
  • Sound.xyz: Also dead. Went offline January 16, 2026. Team is now building Vault.fm (closed beta). On-chain collectibles remain accessible via Splits.org for fund withdrawals, but the storefront is gone. Two major web3 music platforms down in 2026. (sound.xyz)
  • Vocana: Still in public beta as an indie-only streaming platform with user-centric payout model. Forbes specifically named it as a "specialized music service" to watch in 2026. Partnership with Symphonic for early artist access. The question remains whether user-centric royalties actually attract listeners or just attract artists who want fairer math. (vocana.co, forbes.com)
  • Mirlo: Open-source, federated Bandcamp alternative. Interview on DIY Conspiracy highlights its self-hosting philosophy and interoperability focus. Explicitly recommends Faircamp for artists who want full independence. Still early, still niche, but the federated model is genuinely different from everything else. (diyconspiracy.net)
  • Faircamp: Highlighted in Hypebot's "7 Platforms That Put Artists Back in Charge" as the most radical option — not a marketplace, just a static site generator. Free, nonprofit, no account, no central catalog. The "artist becomes the platform" play. Requires technical chops that most artists don't have. (hypebot.com)

Fediverse & decentralized

  • Mirlo's federated model is the only ActivityPub-adjacent music distribution story with momentum right now. Funkwhale had no notable news this week. PeerTube music channels remain a niche curiosity. The fediverse music stack is still more theory than practice.

Streaming economics

  • Luminate 2026 Midyear Report (the big one this week): Global on-demand audio streams hit 2.8 trillion in H1 2026, up 9.8% YoY. US growth slower at 4.8%. Dance/Electronic is the top US growth genre. English-language streaming share fell to a historic low of 87.1% in the US; Spanish-language streams now represent 9.4%. CD sales surged 16% (6.7% ex-K-pop). Indie sector growing streaming share in mid-to-lower volume tiers. The macro story: streaming growth is accelerating globally but flattening in the US, and physical formats are not dying. (luminatedata.com, apnews.com)
  • Spotify: Claims $11B in 2025 payouts. Per-stream rate still $0.003–$0.005. Minimum payout threshold of €50 remains in effect. The payout numbers are real but the per-stream math hasn't moved. Putting on an R&B festival in Dallas ("RNB X Live") — the platform-as-promoter strategy continues. (newsroom.spotify.com, artyfile.com)
  • Forbes analysis (Bill Rosenblatt): Argues 2026 is the inflection year where streaming's dominance levels off in Western markets. US streaming revenue was flat (inflation-adjusted) 2023–H1 2025, total revenue actually down 1.7% H1 2024→H1 2025. AI-generated tracks are flooding services — Deezer reports 50K/day pure AI tracks by late 2025, with 70% of fraudulent streams being AI tracks. At current pace, AI-generated tracks will outnumber human-created ones by mid-2026. Predicts rise of curated/specialized services. (forbes.com)

Industry pulse

  • Wixen v. Meta dismissed (for now): Federal judge tossed Wixen's $100M+ copyright infringement lawsuit against Meta for failing to adequately allege standing under the Copyright Act. Key holding: a copyright co-owner can't unilaterally grant exclusive licenses that override other co-owners' rights. Wixen has until September 18 to file an amended complaint. The defamation and tort claims weren't addressed. Meta is also fighting suits from Epidemic Sound and Eminem's publisher. This matters because it's the highest-profile test of whether publishers can force platforms into better licensing — and the answer so far is "not easily." (digitalmusicnews.com)
  • AI music in the charts: Luminate reports the highest-ranking AI-assisted song globally is Chill77 & Unjaps' "Papaoutai (Afro Soul)" at #282 with 210.7M global audio streams in H1 2026. Not a takeover, but a foothold. Meanwhile, 54% of US musicians express positive feelings toward AI tools; only 35% of non-musicians agree. The artist community is more AI-curious than the general public.
  • CD surge: 16% growth in US CD sales, 6.7% ex-K-pop. This isn't just collectible K-pop — physical is genuinely back as a superfandom channel. Vinyl only grew 2.4%.

Unstream implications

  • The web3 music shakeout is real. Nina and Sound.xyz both gone. Vault.fm (Sound's successor) is the only web3-adjacent project still standing, and it's pivoting away from the public-facing storefront model. Unstream should not chase blockchain distribution — the market has spoken.
  • SoundCloud selling music directly is a competitive signal. If SoundCloud becomes a Bandcamp competitor with 190+ countries of reach, that's the most credible threat to Bandcamp's core business since Songtradr. Unstream should track the revenue split when it's published and consider whether SoundCloud's storefront features affect where indie artists spend their energy.
  • CD and physical are a superfandom channel, not nostalgia. 16% CD growth, Luminate data showing 20% of US music listeners qualify as superfans who engage 5+ ways — physical merchandise is a superfan product. If Unstream has any physical fulfillment plans, the data supports it.
  • AI content flooding is an opportunity for curated platforms. Deezer's data (50K AI tracks/day, 70% of fraud is AI-generated) means discoverability on algorithmic platforms is about to get worse. Platforms that promise human-only catalogs have a selling point. Unstream's value proposition should lean hard into "no AI spam, real artists only."
  • Spanish-language content is a growth vector. 9.4% of US streams, historic low for English, Latin genres climbing. If Unstream's catalog strategy isn't considering bilingual/Latino artists, it should.

Confidence

  • Confirmed: Luminate data, Wixen dismissal order, Nina/Sound.xyz shutdowns, SoundCloud direct sales beta, Vocana public beta, Ampwall pricing/stats, Bandcamp Friday dates.
  • Informed analysis: Forbes Rosenblatt piece on streaming plateau is well-sourced editorial opinion; the US revenue flatline claim cites RIAA data but I haven't independently verified the inflation-adjusted numbers. Deezer's AI track counts are self-reported.
  • Speculation: Whether SoundCloud's direct sales will meaningfully compete with Bandcamp, whether Vault.fm will ship anything meaningful, whether Vocana will find an audience beyond artists who want fairer payout math.
