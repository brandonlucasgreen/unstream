Weekly Unstream Dispatch, week of September 14

Platform watch

  • Bandcamp: Business as usual. September Bandcamp Friday came and went (Sept 4). Editorial still running daily. Total fan payouts crossed $1.8B. No product or policy changes detected this week. Songtradr ownership remains quiet — the Reddit speculation about Bandcamp's future hinges on AI gutting Songtradr's sync business, but that's still conjecture. 15% digital cut remains the standard.
  • Ampwall: Profiled in Hypebot's "7 Platforms That Put Artists Back in Charge" (Sep 4). Still $10/year for 5 hours of audio, 5% transaction cut. Public benefit company, no ads/crypto/AI music. 1,000+ artists. Community-governed feature pipeline. Solid niche, still small.
  • Mirlo: Big move — FairPlayer integration went live Aug 10. Mirlo artists can now opt into federated streaming via FairPlayer, which aggregates music from Mirlo and Faircamp into a single browsable player. Artists control how many free plays before a purchase prompt. This is the most concrete step toward a federated indie music ecosystem any platform has taken. Mirlo is organized as a worker co-op and open source.
  • Faircamp: Still the most radical option — free, nonprofit static site generator. No central catalog, no marketplace, no platform lock-in. You host it yourself, you connect your own payment tools. Featured alongside Mirlo and Ampwall in Hypebot's roundup.

Emerging & alternative

  • Vocana: Still in public beta. User-centric royalty model (your subscription dollars go to artists you actually listen to). Partnered with MassiveMusic and Symphonic for onboarding. Press page shows coverage from Forbes, Music Ally, TechRadar, Attack Magazine — decent media footprint for a platform that hasn't formally launched yet. No hard launch date or funding news this week.
  • Subvert: Profiled in Hypebot's Sept 4 piece. Marketplace collectively owned by artists/labels/supporters/workers. 0% platform fee — buyers pay, sellers keep purchase price after processing. Optional contributions from buyers. One-member-one-vote governance. Still early; vapor risk is real but the structure is interesting.
  • EVEN: Sells early access and bundles (music, merch, memberships) at artist-set prices. 20% digital cut, 15% memberships, 5% merch. Not a streaming alternative — more like a direct-to-fan storefront that treats streaming as the end of a release cycle. Luminate-reportable for chart eligibility.
  • Sleeve: Artist website builder that also handles email lists, stores, memberships. No platform fee (payment processing still applies). Free tier up to 250 email subscribers. Interesting for Unstream as a data-ownership play.
  • Audius: Hypebot profile notes 250M+ streams and millions of monthly users. Still the only decentralized streaming platform with real scale. $AUDIO token remains a learning curve for non-crypto artists. Proves the model can attract users — doesn't prove it can pay artists well.

Fediverse & decentralized

  • FairPlayer + Mirlo: The FairPlayer integration is the week's most significant fediverse development. It's a federated streaming layer that makes music from Mirlo and Faircamp discoverable in one player — no central platform required. Artists opt in and set free-play limits. This is the architecture that ActivityPub music projects have been theorizing about; someone actually shipped it.
  • Faircamp: Still the go-to for self-hosters. No central platform to federate from, but FairPlayer can discover Faircamp sites, making it the decentralized catalog layer.

Streaming economics

  • Spotify: Loud & Clear 2026 update shows $11B+ paid out in 2025, lifetime total $70B. 13,800+ artists at $100K+/year. The 100,000th artist earned $7,300 — up from $350 a decade ago, which sounds great until you realize that's still poverty-level income. Spotify also removed 75 million AI/spam tracks over the past year as part of its fraud crackdown. Meanwhile, Q2 earnings showed 14% revenue growth but a weaker Q3 profit forecast — Spotify is spending heavily on AI features and marketing. Premium subs hit 293M, MAUs 761M.
  • Streaming fraud is now a $2B/year problem. IFPI launched the Streaming Integrity Initiative on Sep 14, backed by 24 companies (Sony, UMG, Warner, RouteNote, FUGA, CD Baby, etc.). Five commitments: verify rights/KYC, vet content for fraud and AI risks, act against repeat offenders, share intelligence, and continuously strengthen detection. This is the first coordinated, cross-industry baseline — and notably, DistroKid did NOT sign on.
  • Deezer: AI-generated music exceeded 50% of daily uploads by June 2026, averaging 90,000 AI tracks/day. But only 1-3% of actual streams. 85% of AI track streams were flagged as fraudulent and demonetized.
  • Luminate: ~150,000 tracks uploaded daily across platforms. 20% of all music on streaming services has received almost no listenership. Luminate is developing in-house AI detection, taking aggregated signals from Apple, Spotify, and Deezer.
  • UMG sues DistroKid (Sep 15): Federal lawsuit alleging deceptive trade practices and copyright infringement. UMG calls DistroKid an "AI slop pipeline," citing 1,000 specific infringements including unauthorized remixes of Ellie Goulding and Sam Smith tracks. Seeking up to $150K per work in statutory damages. DistroKid says it "strongly disagrees." This is the week's biggest story — it directly targets the largest indie distributor's role in flooding platforms.
  • Apple Music: iOS 27 adds custom artist logos and color themes for artist pages. Small but meaningful for brand identity on a platform that's historically been rigid about visual presentation.
  • RIAA Mid-Year 2026: US recorded music hit $6B in H1, up 6.9% YoY. Streaming $4.9B (82%), paid subs $3.4B (+6.4%), physical jumped 25.9% (vinyl +17.7%, CDs +58.6%). 90M paid subscriptions, a record. Sync up 18.2%.
  • YouTube Music: Foundry Class of 2026 expanded to 40 artists from 14 countries. Grants + partner support. Still one of the few DSPs investing directly in independent artist development.

Industry pulse

  • Streaming fraud goes mainstream: The FT, IFPI, and UMG's lawsuit all landed in the same week. The narrative has shifted from "emerging problem" to "coordinated industry response." ~10% of streams are estimated fraudulent, costing ~$2B/year. The industry is no longer just talking — it's suing, organizing, and legislating.
  • AI music volume is staggering but consumption is low: Deezer's data is the clearest snapshot: AI tracks are half of uploads but 1-3% of streams. The real problem isn't AI music finding an audience — it's fraud bots inflating AI tracks to siphon royalties. The distinction matters for policy.
  • German court rules Suno infringed copyright (July 2026): A German court found Suno liable for copyright infringement. Still early, but it's the first major jurisdiction ruling against an AI music generator on copyright grounds.
  • Artists and songwriters push back on AI opt-in: A global coalition of artists, songwriters, and managers released a statement insisting on consent-based AI licensing: no default opt-ins, no terms imposed without genuine choice. This tension between "AI deals" and "artist consent" is going to escalate.
  • Spotify's AI spending squeeze: Q3 profit forecast came in below expectations as Spotify invests in AI features. The bet is that AI-driven recommendations and tools will drive engagement; the risk is that it cannibalizes margins without improving per-stream payouts for artists.

Unstream implications

  • The Mirlo/FairPlayer federated model is the architecture to watch. FairPlayer aggregating music from self-hosted Faircamp sites and Mirlo into one discoverable player — with artist-controlled free-play limits — is exactly the kind of interoperability Unstream should be thinking about. If Unstream isn't planning federation/ActivityPub integration, now is the time. The indie ecosystem is clearly moving toward interconnectedness over siloed platforms.
  • "Human-made" is becoming a market differentiator. With AI tracks at 50%+ of uploads and fraud at 10% of streams, verified human artistry has real marketing value. Unstream should consider how to make "human-made" verifiable and visible — not just as a label, but as a trust signal that carries weight with listeners and artists alike. This is Unstream's lane.
  • The DistroKid lawsuit changes the distribution landscape. Whether UMG wins or settles, the pressure on distributors to KYC and vet uploads is going to intensify. Smaller distributors will have to invest in detection. This could push more artists toward direct-to-fan platforms (Bandcamp, Mirlo, Ampwall, Unstream) as the friction of traditional distribution increases.
  • Sleeve's no-fee email list model is worth studying. Artist-owned data, exportable lists, no platform cut. If Unstream doesn't already offer email list management and fan data export, that's table stakes now.
  • Vocana's user-centric model still hasn't launched fully. There's a window here for Unstream to establish its own payout narrative before Vocana goes wide.
  • Spotify's $10/track fraud penalty and 1,000-stream minimum are hurting small artists ($40M/year redirected from long-tail artists). Unstream should explicitly position its payout model as an alternative that doesn't penalize the smallest artists for fraud they didn't commit.
  • Physical is growing fast (vinyl +17.7%, CDs +58.6%). If Unstream has any physical fulfillment or merch integration, now is a good time to highlight it.

Confidence

  • Confirmed: IFPI Streaming Integrity Initiative (press release, Sep 14). UMG v. DistroKid lawsuit (Variety, Music Week, Sep 15). Spotify 75M track removal (Spotify exec statement). Deezer AI upload stats (Deezer press releases, Apr & Jul 2026). Luminate 150K daily tracks (Billboard podcast, Sep 17). RIAA mid-year figures (official report, Sep 1). Mirlo/FairPlayer integration (Mirlo blog, Aug 10). Apple Music iOS 27 artist customization (RouteNote, Sep 16). YouTube Music Foundry expansion (YouTube blog, Sep 17).
  • Well-sourced but interpretive: The $2B/year fraud estimate (IFPI + Beatdapp, cross-referenced with Chartlex analysis). Spotify's Q3 profit outlook (Reuters, Yahoo Finance). Vocana's market position (multiple press mentions but no hard launch metrics).
  • Speculation/rumor: Reddit speculation about Bandcamp's future under Songtradr. Whether DistroKid will settle or fight the UMG lawsuit. The actual consumer appetite for user-centric streaming models (Vocana's bet). Whether FairPlayer's federated approach will gain meaningful listener adoption.
