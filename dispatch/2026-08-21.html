Weekly Unstream Dispatch, week of August 21

August 21, 2026

Platform watch

  • Bandcamp: Quiet week. Bandcamp Friday ran August 7 with no ownership or policy changes to report. The Songtradr era continues to be stable-status-quo — no new controversy, no new features. Bandcamp's Daily editorial is publishing as normal. The platform remains the default "sell your music" option for indies but is losing ground to alternatives that offer better UI, higher audio quality, and no AI-ties baggage.
  • Mirlo: Growing. A December 2025 in-depth interview with DIY Conspiracy confirms Mirlo is run as a worker co-op (unpaid volunteers still, but structurally solid). They've nearly reached feature parity with Bandcamp's core digital-store functionality and are now pivoting toward federation and decentralization — the next phase. Mirlo's default take is 10%, but artists can set it to 0%. Notably, in mid-August Mirlo posted about FairPlayer, a new discovery tool that aggregates music from both Mirlo *and* Faircamp artists, hinting at cross-platform federation. This is the most interesting platform-level development this week for Unstream's ecosystem.
  • Ampwall: Still active and positioning itself as the audiophile's Bandcamp alternative. A July 2026 artist post highlights two selling points: 24-bit/48kHz upload and download support (no quality degradation), and an anti-AI stance. "We share similar values" on AI, says the artist. Ampwall's fee structure and modern UI continue to attract artists who've left Bandcamp. Low awareness outside niche circles but strong loyalty among users.
  • Patreon / Qobuz / Beatport / Discords: Nothing significant this week. No policy shifts, no pricing changes detected.

Emerging & alternative

  • Sound.xyz: Dead. The site now displays a notice: "Sound.xyz is now offline as of January 16, 2026." The web3 music minting experiment is over. Another reminder that crypto-native music platforms have an extremely high failure rate.
  • Nina Protocol: Winding down. The Solana-based music protocol confirmed it is sunsetting. Existing releases remain accessible but no new activity is being onboarded. Two major web3 music platforms exiting in 2026 — the space is consolidating hard toward traditional direct-to-fan models.
  • Vocana: A Forbes piece from late 2025 identified Vocana as a new entrant in the "specialized music services" space, focused on indie artists, currently in beta. No fresh news this week. Still worth watching but no adoption signals yet.
  • Ampled / Resonate: Both confirmed closed/shuttered in Mirlo's interview — they're cited as predecessors whose lessons informed Mirlo's design. Not returning.
  • Audius: Still operational, no meaningful news this week.

Fediverse & decentralized

  • Mirlo + Faircamp federation: The biggest signal this week. Mirlo's Instagram post about "FairPlayer" — a discovery tool that pulls artists from both Mirlo *and* Faircamp (the static-site, self-hosted Bandcamp alternative) — is the first real move toward cross-platform music discovery in the fediverse/indie ecosystem. If this works, it proves that federated music discovery doesn't need a single platform.
  • Funkwhale: No new developments this week. Still active as a federated (ActivityPub) music hosting project, but adoption remains small and development is slow. The Reddit-fediverse thread about wanting a "federated Bandcamp" confirms ongoing demand but Funkwhale isn't positioned to fill it — it's more of a self-hosted Spotify than a Bandcamp.

Streaming economics

  • Spotify — "AI Persona" label (Aug 11): Starting mid-September, Spotify will badge AI-generated artist profiles with an "AI Persona" label. These profiles will be excluded from algorithmic recommendations. Spotify will allow self-disclosure but will also apply the label via human moderators using AI detection tools. This is a meaningful move: it functionally quarantines AI slop from the discovery pipeline. Confirmed reporting. Variety
  • Apple Music — "Made With AI" labels (Aug 20): Apple Music will begin labeling songs where "a material portion of the content" was created using AI. Unlike Tidal's approach (which bans royalties for AI content), Apple places the disclosure burden on labels and distributors. Apple's VP Oliver Schusser said earlier this year that "more than a third" of uploads are "100% AI." Confirmed. Variety
  • Tidal — AI music ban from royalties (June 29, policy effective July 15): Tidal will label AI-generated music and ban royalty payouts for wholly AI-generated tracks. The most aggressive stance of any major streamer. Tidal receives "an overwhelming amount of AI-generated music from 3rd party distributors," per Tidal lead Robert Andersen. Confirmed. Variety
  • RIAA/IFPI/Grammys/SAG-AFTRA — Unified AI labeling framework (July 10): A coalition of major industry bodies introduced two track-level labels: "AI-Generated" and "AI-Assisted." Voluntary framework. Spotify and Apple Music are taking their own approaches rather than adopting this exactly. Deezer welcomed it but didn't commit. The DiMA (streaming services' trade group) said it's watching closely. Confirmed. Variety

Industry pulse

  • EU AI Act enforcement begins August 2, 2026: This is now live. GPAI providers (Suno, Udio, ElevenLabs, etc.) must publish training data summaries. AI-generated audio distributed in the EU must be machine-detectable and disclosed. Distributors operating in EU markets are accountable at point of upload. Penalties up to €15M or 3% global turnover. Fewer than 8% of indie artists could accurately describe their disclosure obligations, per Chartlex. This is the biggest regulatory story of the year for music and it just went into effect. Chartlex
  • Protect Working Musicians Act (H.R. 8994): Introduced May 21, 2026 by Rep. Deborah Ross (D-NC). Would allow individual music creators earning under $1M/year to collectively negotiate licensing terms with dominant platforms (>$100M revenue) and AI companies. Still in committee. No floor vote scheduled. Congress.gov
  • Udio & GRAI license UMG-backed patents (Aug 20): Music IP Holdings (a UMG/Liquidax joint venture) licensed 24+ patents covering AI music generation, moderation, watermarking, and monetization to Udio and GRAI. This signals UMG's strategy: don't just sue AI companies — build a licensing infrastructure so rightsholders get paid when AI generates derivative works. Exclusive reporting. Variety
  • Latin and country surging: Luminate/RIAA data shows Latin and country accounted for 180.3 billion streams in H1 2026 in the US, followed by rock (137.2B) and pop (87B). Not directly actionable for Unstream but confirms genre diversification continues.

Unstream implications

  • AI transparency is now table stakes. Three major streamers (Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal) have committed to AI labeling in the past 8 weeks, and the EU just made it legally mandatory for EU distribution. Unstream should build AI-disclosure fields into its artist upload flow now — not because Unstream hosts AI content, but because artists distributing through Unstream to Spotify/Apple/Tidal will need to tag correctly or risk being penalized. This is a compliance feature that differentiates from Bandcamp (which has been silent on AI).
  • FairPlayer / cross-platform discovery is the model to study. Mirlo's experiment aggregating discovery across Mirlo + Faircamp is exactly the kind of interoperability Unstream should be thinking about. If Unstream can expose a discoverable feed (RSS, ActivityPub, or even just a well-structured JSON API), it becomes part of this emerging federated discovery layer rather than an isolated silo.
  • Sound.xyz and Nina Protocol are both dead. The web3 music era is over. Don't build blockchain infrastructure. Build cooperative, open-standards infrastructure instead. Mirlo's co-op model is the one with momentum.
  • Tidal's "no royalties for AI" stance is the boldest. If Unstream wants to position as artist-first, a clear policy on AI-generated content — even a simple one like requiring human-creation disclosure — would be on brand. Tidal's approach (ban AI royalties entirely) is extreme but resonates with Ampwall's positioning too. The market is clearly splitting between "AI is fine, just label it" (Spotify/Apple) and "AI content doesn't deserve royalties" (Tidal). Unstream should pick a side and state it.
  • EU AI Act enforcement is now live. If Unstream has any EU-bound distribution or artists, the August 2 compliance deadline has passed. Unstream should consider publishing a brief guide for artists about EU AI disclosure requirements — it's a content marketing opportunity and a genuine service to artists who (per Chartlex) overwhelmingly don't understand the new rules.

Confidence

  • Confirmed: Spotify AI Persona label (mid-September rollout), Apple Music "Made With AI" labels, Tidal AI royalty ban, RIAA/IFPI AI labeling framework, EU AI Act enforcement date, Sound.xyz closure, Nina Protocol wind-down, Udio/GRAI patent licensing, Mirlo FairPlayer discovery feature, Protect Working Musicians Act introduction.
